## 1. MARKET RESEARCH

### 1.1 Global Travel Market

- Adventure/experiential travel growing at **15–17% CAGR** through 2030 — one of the fastest-growing consumer markets globally
- **Soft adventure tourism** (island hopping, kayaking, snorkeling, cultural tours) = **64.2% of the market** — exactly what Laagan Adventure sells
- **Couples are the largest segment** at 42.1% of adventure travelers — key marketing target
- Gen Z: 50% cite local cultural immersion as top motivator. TikTok drove 29% of Gen Z destination discovery in 2023
- Post-COVID trend: travelers are re-prioritizing experiences over material goods — a structural shift, not a temporary surge

- Sub-segment C: OFWs and diaspora — higher disposable income, emotional motivation to "show their city," natural referral network for international visitors

- Age 22–45

## 2. UI/UX RESEARCH

### 2.1 Hero Section Best Practices

**What converts (G Adventures, Intrepid, Trafalgar model — right for Laagan Adventure):**
- Full-viewport cinematic image or muted looping video — video boosts conversion up to 80%, increases time-on-page 20%+
- For mobile: video falls back to optimized WebP/AVIF static image below 768px. Video file must be under 5MB
- Story-led hero: bold photography of real people in real moments (not stock)
- One aspirational headline (3–6 words), one-line subheadline, one CTA button — nothing else competes
- Semi-transparent dark scrim (rgba 0,0,0 at 0.35–0.5) ensures WCAG AA text contrast
- **Don't use the Airbnb model** (search bar as hero) — that's for comparison shoppers, not destination discovery

**Recommended for Laagan Adventure:**
- Full-viewport pink sand / vinta video (muted, looped, 20–30 sec)
- Headline: **"The Only Pink Sand Beach in Asia."**
- Subline: "Guided island adventures in Zamboanga City."
- One CTA: "See Our Tours"
- No booking widget in hero — establish desire first, then book

### 2.2 Tour Cards (Listing Pages)

Standard anatomy that converts:
1. **16:9 or 3:2 image**, full-bleed, no clipping
2. **Badge overlay** top-left: "Best Seller", "Only 4 spots left", "National Geographic Pick"
3. **Tour name** (2 lines max), star rating + review count, duration, price per person
4. **Price anchoring**: show per-person price prominently — never bury it
5. **Hover state** (desktop): brief description appears as mini-preview
6. **Grid**: 2 columns tablet, single column mobile — vertical scroll only, no horizontal

**What 57% of sites get wrong:** No map, no visible meeting point — visitors left uncertain about logistics.

### 2.3 Tour Detail Page Structure

Optimal top-to-bottom structure:
1. **Gallery** — 4–8 images, swipeable carousel on mobile, grid lightbox on desktop. Real guest photos outperform professional shots for trust.
2. **Title + metadata bar** — Duration / Group size / Departure point — as icon+label chips, not paragraphs
3. **Price + sticky CTA** — sticky at mobile bottom throughout page. "Check Availability" performs better than "Book Now" on first exposure (lower commitment, higher click)
4. **Overview** — 100–150 words, second person ("You'll..."), sensory language
5. **Itinerary accordion** — day-by-day with inclusions and exclusions as two clearly labeled lists
6. **Practical info accordion** — What to bring, what to wear, meeting point with embedded map
7. **Cancellation policy** — visible, plain-language, NOT buried. "Free cancellation before [date]" pattern (Airbnb)
8. **Reviews** — min 3, with star rating, name, date, photo. TripAdvisor/Facebook badge linking to verified profile
9. **Related tours** — 2–3 cards at bottom, keep user in funnel

108.6% higher conversion when users engage with reviews on detail pages.

### 2.4 Booking Flow

3–4 steps maximum. Every extra step adds abandonment.

- Step 1: Select date (calendar) + group size (stepper, NOT dropdown)
- Step 2: Review summary (tour, date, group, price breakdown, cancellation reminder)
- Step 3: Contact details (Name, Email, Phone — minimum only)
- Step 4: Payment

**Rules:**
- Visible step counter ("Step 2 of 4") reduces abandonment
- Price shown must equal final price — no surprises at checkout
- Trust signals AT the payment button: GCash logo, lock icon, cancellation policy summary
- 19% of booking abandonments are caused by trust concerns at the payment step

### 2.5 Filter/Search UX

- Mobile: filters open in a **bottom sheet drawer** — never a sidebar (sidebar is desktop only)
- Applied filters show as removable chip tags at top of results
- Results count updates live ("Showing 4 tours")
- "Reset all filters" always accessible
- Filter taxonomy for Laagan Adventure: Activity type / Duration / Price range / Group size / Date availability

### 2.6 Trust Signal Hierarchy (by conversion impact)

1. Review ratings (star + count) — on cards AND detail pages
2. Third-party badges (TripAdvisor, Google) — with clickable links to verified profiles
3. Media mentions / awards — "National Geographic Best Beach" — near or in hero
4. Cancellation guarantee — visible before price commitment
5. Payment security badges — at checkout only
6. Guide profiles — name, face, credentials — humanizes the transaction
7. Real traveler photos (UGC) — not stock, not polished

Research: 3–5 curated homepage testimonials is optimal. 100+ reviews in the library (detail pages) correlates with 37% higher conversions.

### 2.7 Philippine-Specific UX Patterns

**Cultural design values:**

- **Pakikipagkapwa** (shared identity): "Maria, Cebu City" outperforms an anonymous testimonial. Social proof from fellow Filipinos converts better.
- **Malasakit** (compassionate care): Warm error messages, not technical ones. "We couldn't find tours for those dates — try a nearby date or message us directly." A visible human contact button signals care.
- **Praktikalidad** (practicality): "What's included?" and "No hidden fees" are trust signals, not just utility. Price transparency = trust.

**PH-specific patterns:**
- Persistent WhatsApp/Messenger button — expected and trusted more than a contact form
- Facebook reviews widget — carries more local weight than TripAdvisor for domestic audience
- GCash first in payment flow — 94M registered users, 89% mobile wallet market share
- Instagram/Facebook live feed — real recent activity signals legitimacy
- Taglish microcopy: "I-book na!" / "Tara na!" can outperform pure English CTAs for domestic audience

**Performance floor for PH mobile:**
- Page load under 3 seconds on 4G — 53% of users abandon above this threshold
- Average PH mobile speed: ~32 Mbps (79th globally) — outside Metro Manila, 3G is common
- Test on budget Android (2–4GB RAM), not flagship phones
- All images via `next/image` — automatic WebP/AVIF, lazy loading, responsive sizes
- LCP under 2.5 seconds — hero image must use `priority` prop

### 2.8 Mobile UX — Thumb Zone Design

- **Green zone** (lower center/right): Primary CTAs — "Book Now," "Next," "Confirm" must live here
- **Yellow zone** (upper/lower edges): Secondary actions
- **Red zone** (upper corners): Never place primary CTAs here

**Applications for Laagan Adventure:**
- Tour detail page CTA: bottom-fixed sticky bar, full width, 56px minimum height
- Navigation menu trigger: lower-right (not upper-left)
- Filter button: bottom center of listing pages
- Carousel: swipe gestures, not small arrow taps
- All form fields: 44px minimum height, correct keyboard type (`type="tel"`, `type="email"`)
- Group size selector: stepper (- / number / +), NOT a dropdown

### 2.9 Conversion Optimization

**CTA text hierarchy:**
- "Check Availability" — best (lowest commitment, highest click rate)
- "Reserve My Spot" — better (implies scarcity, creates ownership)
- "Book Now" — acceptable but generic

**CTA placement on tour detail page:**
1. Above the fold in detail hero
2. Sticky at mobile bottom throughout
3. After tour description
4. After reviews (second peak of buying intent)
5. Bottom of page

**Authentic scarcity (Laagan Adventure has it, use it honestly):**
- "Only 400 visitors allowed on Santa Cruz Island per day" — DOT/DENR policy, not a sales tactic
- "X spots remaining for [date]" — if booking system tracks capacity
- "Last booked 2 hours ago" — if submittedAt data is available
- Never: fake countdown timers — Filipinos detect manufactured urgency, and it destroys trust

**Price framing:**
- Show full experience → inclusions → then price (value before cost)
- Per-person breakdown: "From P100/person" beats "P1,000 for the boat"
- GCash installment: "Pay as low as P200/month via GCash GGives"
- Online booking discount: "Normally P1,500 — Book online at P1,200"

### 2.10 Emotional Design — The 10-Second Journey

**Designed for Laagan Adventure:**

| Second | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| 0–2s | Full-viewport vinta video or pink sand image loads. Color is warm, saturated, unfamiliar. Brain signals: "I haven't seen this before." |
| 2–4s | Headline reads: "The Only Pink Sand Beach in Asia." Subline: "Guided island adventures in Zamboanga City." User understands where, what, and why it's special. |
| 4–6s | Eye drops to CTA: "See Our Tours." Clean, obvious, no friction. |
| 6–8s | Peripheral awareness of star rating + review count, or "National Geographic" logo. Trust established without requiring a click. |
| 8–10s | Decision: stay or leave. |

**Visual triggers that work:**
- People IN the experience (not empty landscapes) — viewer projects themselves into the scene
- Movement (vinta sailing, person diving, jellyfish drifting)
- Authentic, not stock — Filipinos are highly attuned to inauthenticity
- Slightly warm, saturated color grading — the pink sand palette is naturally high-saturation

**Copy patterns for aspiration:**
- Second-person, present-tense: "You're sailing on a vinta, watching the pink sand disappear behind you."
- Sensory: "Pink sand that only exists in one place in Asia." "Jellyfish that can't sting you, drifting around your feet."
- Exclusivity: "Only 400 visitors per day. Most people will never see this place."

### 2.11 Homepage Conversion Architecture

Engineered as a funnel, not a brochure:

1. **Hero** — Emotional desire. CTA: "See Our Tours."
2. **Social proof bar** — 3 numbers: tours completed / 5-star reviews / years operating
3. **Tour cards** — 3 featured tours. CTA: "Check Availability."
4. **Destination story** — "Why Zamboanga?" — pink sand, National Geographic, visitor cap
5. **Trust signals** — DOT accreditation, media mentions, review badge
6. **Testimonials** — 3 curated reviews with faces, names, origin cities (Filipino cities)
7. **FAQ teaser** — 3 common questions. Reduces hesitation before committing.
8. **Final CTA** — Full-width section before footer: "Ready? Book your adventure today."

### 3.3 JSON-LD to Add (SEO — High Impact)

**TouristTrip schema** on every tour detail page:
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "TouristTrip",
  "name": "[tour.title]",
  "description": "[tour.description]",
  "image": "[urlFor(tour.mainImage).url()]",
  "provider": {
    "@type": "TouristInformationCenter",
    "name": "Laagan Adventure",
    "url": "https://laagan-adventure.vercel.app",
    "telephone": "+639052435196",
    "address": {
      "@type": "PostalAddress",
      "addressLocality": "Zamboanga City",
      "addressCountry": "PH"
    }
  },
  "offers": {
    "@type": "Offer",
    "price": "[tour.price]",
    "priceCurrency": "PHP"
  }
}
```

**LocalBusiness schema** in `layout.tsx` (site-wide):
```json
{
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "Laagan Adventure",
  "priceRange": "₱₱",
  "openingHours": "Mo-Su 06:00-20:00",
  "geo": {
    "@type": "GeoCoordinates",
    "latitude": 6.9214,
    "longitude": 122.0790
  }
}
```

**FAQPage schema** on tour pages — Google shows FAQ entries as expandable rich snippets in search results. Free SERP real estate.

**BlogPosting schema** on blog posts — helps posts rank for featured snippets.
